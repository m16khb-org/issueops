# Agent-evaluation reliability and cost measurement

**Retrieved:** 2026-10-02
**Scope:** One bounded evidence lane for IssueOps performance, optimization, and visibility. Public benchmark claims below are not IssueOps measurements.

## Findings

1. **A benchmark score can be wrong in both directions: contamination inflates it, while broken tasks and tests suppress it.** OpenAI’s review of SWE-bench Verified (500 tasks, originally released August 2024) audited 138 often-failed problems: it reports material test or statement flaws in 59.4%, and says tested frontier models reproduced gold patches or task-specific details. OpenAI consequently stopped reporting Verified scores and recommends SWE-bench Pro while it develops less-contaminated evaluations. This is an official, single-publisher audit, not an independent replication; the fetched article did not expose its publication date or a newer benchmark version. **Certainty:** high that OpenAI reports these findings; not independently established here that all affected models or benchmarks share them. **Applicability:** treat public task-set scores as a dated signal, retain task/version identifiers, inspect suspicious successes and failures, and use fresh or private tasks for regression checks where feasible. **Counterevidence:** Verified was curated by three experts per problem from a larger pool, and OpenAI says Pro is not contamination-free either. Source: [OpenAI, “Why SWE-bench Verified no longer measures frontier coding capabilities”](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/).

2. **Agent benchmark infrastructure is part of the treatment, not neutral plumbing.** Anthropic’s 2026-02-05 engineering report (date listed in its [Engineering index](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering)) on Terminal-Bench 2.0 found a six-percentage-point spread across resource configurations, with infra failures falling from 5.8% under strict limits to 0.5% uncapped. The authors distinguish reliability headroom from extra resources that enable new solution strategies; on their SWE-bench crossover (227 problems, 10 samples each), five-times RAM raised scores 1.54 points. These are Anthropic’s internal experiments, one official source, not independently replicated results. **Certainty:** high as a report of those experiments; transfer magnitude is uncertain. **Applicability:** pin and report task-set version, harness, hardware/resource floor and kill ceiling, timeout, concurrency, retries, and infra-error rate; rerun close comparisons under matched settings. **Counterevidence:** on their Terminal-Bench run, 1x-to-3x score variation was statistically indistinguishable from noise (p=0.40), even as infra errors improved. The 3x boundary is benchmark-specific, not a universal setting. Source: [Anthropic, “Quantifying infrastructure noise in agentic coding evals” (2026-02-05)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise).

3. **Report quality and cost together, with a clearly named claim and budget.** OpenAI’s third-party-evaluation playbook says controlled comparisons need a shared task/scoring setup and fixed budget; reports should disclose harness, tools, tokens, cost, time, and validity checks. It recommends expected cost per successful solve when repeated attempts are relevant. Separately, METR’s **Expenditure Horizon** (2026-07-21) demonstrates cost-performance curves including API inference, experiment compute, and human labor, then revalidates candidate gains. METR reports that compute consumed about 70–90% of most NanoGPT agent-run costs and that raw trajectories overstated progress due to noise; its human-cost baseline is explicitly uncertain. These independent organizations support complementary measurement principles, but neither independently reproduces the other’s numbers. **Certainty:** high for the published guidance and study details; IssueOps impact is unmeasured. **Applicability:** capture success/quality, attempts, elapsed time, model-reported token usage and provider cost when available, plus non-model compute; compare cost per accepted success, not tokens alone. **Counterevidence:** METR’s human labor estimates and benchmark transfer are uncertain, and it discloses possible model training exposure to the optimization problem. Sources: [OpenAI, “A shared playbook for trustworthy third party evaluations”](https://openai.com/index/trustworthy-third-party-evaluations-foundations/); [METR, “Expenditure Horizon” (2026-07-21)](https://metr.org/blog/2026-07-21-expenditure-horizon/).

4. **IssueOps already separates repeated-run reliability from deterministic scorer checks, but its benchmark result contract does not expose cost or resource accounting.** `internal/contract/issueopsbenchmark/benchmark_run_types.go:22-30` records fixture counts and scores without token, dollar, duration, or infrastructure fields. `internal/domain/issueopsbenchmark/issueops_reliability.go:14-33` explicitly rejects rescoring one artifact as independent trials and requires distinct run IDs, provenance, and aligned fixtures; it requires at least two runs, with acceptance using k>=3. `internal/contract/issueopsbenchmark/benchmark_run_types.go:72-78` and `internal/domain/issueopsbenchmark/issueops_self_consistency.go:10-24` caution that provenance guards do not prove independent execution and that consensus reduces verdict variance, not judge bias. **Certainty:** high for the cited implementation. **Applicability:** extend visibility only at a real agent/provider execution boundary that can report usage; keep unavailable cost fields absent rather than fabricated. Track infra failures separately from agent failures. **Counterevidence:** this bounded lane did not audit all telemetry or host adapters, so these DTOs do not establish that no other duration or usage reporting exists.

## Source classification

OpenAI and Anthropic claims are official single-source claims about their own analyses. METR is an independent publisher and supplies a separate cost/revalidation example, not a replication of their measurements. No independent reproduction of the cited contamination or infrastructure percentages was found in this bounded source set.

## EXPAND

- **Actionable:** inspect actual supported-host usage fields and add cost/duration only where provider records can be attributed to an evaluation run without capturing prompt content.
- **Actionable:** run a small matched IssueOps benchmark with distinct executions, pinned fixtures/harness/resource limits, per-run infra errors, and cost per accepted pass; compare repeated-run intervals before micro-optimizing.
- **Actionable:** maintain a fresh/private regression fixture subset and periodically check public benchmark versions for contamination or scorer drift. No production or configuration changes were made here.
